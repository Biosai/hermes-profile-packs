# Venues and on-ramps

What to compare, and what to check, before proposing a venue. Names are examples of a category, not recommendations; fees and licences change, so read them from the provider's own pages each time and give the date.

## Decentralised venues
- **On-chain order books** (a dedicated chain or rollup with a central limit order book) feel closest to an exchange: maker and taker fees, limit orders, mobile access through a wallet browser. Check where collateral is bridged from and whether the venue is licensed where the owner lives (usually not).
- **Aggregators and swap protocols** suit occasional swaps and consolidating long-tail tokens. Prefer one with protection against front-running for larger swaps.
- No decentralised venue moves fiat: pair it with a regulated on-ramp.

## Fiat on-ramps and off-ramps
Compare, for a person and for a company separately:
- **Licence** where the owner lives: in the EU, the authorisation as a crypto-asset service provider in the ESMA register, and the home regulator.
- **Company accounts**: whether the provider onboards companies, and what it asks (registry extract, beneficial owners, accountant's letter).
- **Fees from the fee page**: maker and taker on the exact pair, the spread on instant "buy", deposit and withdrawal fees per method and network. The trading screen is usually cheaper than instant buy.
- **Euro stablecoin route**: bank transfer in, convert to a regulated euro stablecoin on a low-fee pair, withdraw on chain. Often the cheapest route; check the pair's fee.
- **Account-linked stablecoin issuers** give an IBAN tied to a wallet and mint or redeem a euro stablecoin; check the issuer's fees and whether it onboards companies.

## Before naming a venue
1. Licence status read on the regulator's register, with the date.
2. Fees read on the provider's own page, with the date.
3. Restrictions for the owner's country read in the provider's own notices; press reports are second-hand.
