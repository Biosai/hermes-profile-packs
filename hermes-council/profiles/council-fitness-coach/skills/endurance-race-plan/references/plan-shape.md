# The plan shape

Every distance, pace and temperature is written in the user's own units.

## Phases
Work back from the event date: base, build, peak, taper.
- After a long gap since the last event, start with a restart week. Run/walk is fine.
- Grow weekly volume gradually, by about ten percent at most.
- Lighten every third or fourth week.
- Keep most sessions easy and conversational. Add one quality session once the base is solid.
- Taper for about the last two weeks before a long event.

## Targets
Turn recent results into paces or efforts, then give two targets: a realistic one and a stretch one, each as a pace and as the percentage it asks over the current result. Decide on the stretch target at a mid-plan checkpoint.

Worked example, a half marathon:

| Finish time | Pace per kilometre | Pace per mile |
|---|---|---|
| 2:10 | 6:10 | 9:55 |
| 2:00 | 5:41 | 9:09 |

Going from 2:10 to 2:00 is about 8% faster. Show the user's own unit only.

## The standard week
- One line per day, with every activity the user counts as training, not only the event's sport.
- Skills and other sports the user enjoys belong in it, each with a ladder of levels and a simple baseline test.
- Each session says what, how long or how far, at what effort, and why.
- The full week-by-week table goes in the training record, not in the reply. Compute the week dates with a script.

## Adapting
- One missed day is noise. Never make it up by doubling the next session.
- After a missed week, repeat the restart week, lighter: two sessions out of three is a good week. Ask what got in the way (time, fatigue, weather, motivation) and fit the plan to the real week.
- When the target no longer fits the date, say so plainly and give options: a slower target, a shorter distance, a later event.
