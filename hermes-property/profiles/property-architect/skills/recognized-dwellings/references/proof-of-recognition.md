# Proof that a dwelling is recognized

## Why this is the question that blocks

A listing that says "four dwellings" says what the seller lets, not what the
municipality authorized. If the investor's strategy requires recognized
dwellings (reselling under option without applying for a permit, for
instance), an unrecognized unit makes that strategy **impossible**, not just
risky. The investor file says which strategies require it; read it.

## Documents, by strength of proof

1. A **permit** issued for that number of dwellings: date, reference, exact
   scope.
2. The municipality's **planning information** (*renseignements
   urbanistiques*). The notary obtains it before the sale; in a public sale it
   is often annexed to the auction conditions (*cahier des charges*).
3. The **land registry** entry as separate dwellings (plots and plans on the
   federal land registry portal).

## What proves nothing

Separate meters, letterboxes, doorbells and running leases **prove nothing**.
The notary generally does not guarantee the number of dwellings: that is the
rule, not the exception. Read what the deed or the auction conditions say
about it.

## Three answers, and only three

Recognized (document quoted), not recognized (a document says so, or the
permit covers fewer units), unknown (no document). **"Unknown" is an answer**:
without a document, the unit comes out unknown; it is not inferred from the
listing, the photos or the number of doorbells.
